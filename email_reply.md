**The per-district picture is worth a look**

The citywide figure hides a lot of movement. Between 2024 and 2026 individual
districts range from -15.4% (D5) to +10.0% (D14). The largest 2024 to 2025
falls are D23 (-13.4%), D5 (-11.8%), D7 (-10.2%), D16 (-8.2%) and D12 (-8.1%).

Those are largely the same districts leading our improvement list. Across all 32:

- Change in cohort size correlates with change in proficiency at r = -0.48, and
  with change in the Level 1 share at r = +0.60.
- The 14 districts whose cohort shrank by more than 5% averaged +4.2pp on
  proficiency and -5.1pp on Level 1.
- The 16 that held steady or grew averaged +0.4pp and -1.3pp.

A district's result is a percentage of whoever sat the test, so a cohort that
changes size by a tenth is a competing explanation for a large measured gain.
This does not make the gains false. It means we want to be able to rule it out
before attributing them to instruction, particularly for D5, D7, D12, D16 and
D23. D14 is a useful counterexample: it grew 10% and still gained 6.9pp.

I have added a change-in-tested column to the district table and a panel
plotting cohort change against proficiency change, so the two are read together.
